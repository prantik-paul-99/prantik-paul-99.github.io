Research

Research that tries to make advanced computing more useful, more secure, and more human-centered.

The current portfolio spans natural language processing, decentralized identity, and machine learning for scientific data processing.

Undergraduate thesis

Common-sense reasoning in Bangla question answering

This NLP-based thesis focused on a Bangla question answering bot capable of reasoning beyond direct context. The work involved building a knowledge graph for Bangla so the system could combine contextual understanding with external common-sense knowledge.

Supervisor: Dr. Rifat Shahriyar

Ongoing research

Self-sovereign identity for online security

This project studies how decentralized systems can support self-sovereign identity, reducing dependence on fragmented platform-specific identities and giving users stronger control over their own data and privacy.

Supervisor: Dr. Md Sadek Ferdous

Independent collaboration

Radio frequency interference mitigation with AI

An ongoing collaborative effort reviewing and organizing machine learning and deep learning methods for removing interference from astronomical telescope data, with the long-term goal of an automated cleaning pipeline.

Area: ML for scientific signal processing

Research themes

The questions underneath the projects

How should systems reason?

Through structured knowledge, contextual interpretation, and models that go beyond shallow pattern matching.

Who controls identity?

Users should have meaningful control over their digital identity and data instead of relying entirely on centralized intermediaries.

Where can AI help directly?

In real systems and data pipelines where automation can improve accuracy, efficiency, and resilience.

For collaboration, mentoring, or discussion around these topics.
